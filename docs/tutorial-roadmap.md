## 3. How a chapter is made

The recipe the first three followed. Chapter 3 (`03-triangulos`) is the model to copy.

1. **Read its entry** in tutorial-v3.md §7, the material it names, and the second version's lesson sheets in
   `docs/tutorial-lessons/` for its subject.
2. **Compute the worked numbers first** (a few lines of `node -e`), before writing figures or text: the defaults of
   each figure, the exercises' answers. The text states only numbers a figure shows or a test checks.
3. **The figures:** one job each (something to drag, a slider, the naive way beside the right one). Use `lab/plane`
   in the plane, `lab` in space. The panel's rows are in English and the tests find them by substring, so no row's
   name may be inside an earlier row's. Numbers come from the state through `derived`, never from inside the draw
   (a frame not drawn would leave them stale). End with a `console.log` hint (the run check needs one line).
4. **The project's step:** only the files it adds or changes. The reader's code is what the chapter explains; what
   only makes the example work is a tool, in `herramientas/` (shown last, read-only). Its unit tests check the
   numbers the text states.
5. **The page:** the opening (where we are on the map, what we will have, the destination image), the story in short
   sections (naive, broken, explained, fixed), a figure every few paragraphs with its `caption`, short exercises after
   each idea (predict, then check; answers in `<details>`), the margins, the **Consigna** right before the project
   (what to change, what to expect, where to look; answers folded), then _Lo que tenemos_ and two or three _Retos_
   with a hint each. Every chapter compares two ways: the library's, Manifold's, or the exact answer.
6. **Link it:** its line on `tutorial/index.mdx` (the "_(todavía no)_" goes), and its entry in tutorial-v3.md §7 if
   the chapter changed it. Update this roadmap's table.
7. **The browser tests** in `e2e/lessons.e2e.ts`: exact for our numbers, a direction for the library's (the canvas's
   numbers are Chrome's and may change with it).
8. **Verify,** in this order and nothing heavy alongside:
   - `npm run format:check`, `npm run lint`, `npm run check`, `npx vitest run`;
   - if `site/src/lab/plane/` changed, `node site/scripts/vendor.mjs` first (`npx astro build` does not run it);
   - build into the scratchpad: `cd site && npx astro build --outDir <scratchpad>/site-dist`;
   - serve it on port 4410 (`npx vite preview --outDir <scratchpad>/site-dist --port 4410 --strictPort --base /Manifold_Gaming/`,
     never the author's dev server on 4321; the phase 5 session uses 4420);
   - run the browser tests with a config copied to the repository's root for the run and deleted after (base URL
     `http://localhost:4410/Manifold_Gaming/`, chromium only, results in the scratchpad), at least
     `e2e/lessons.e2e.ts e2e/tutorial.e2e.ts e2e/navigation.e2e.ts`, and `e2e/site.e2e.ts` when the home page or
     the first page changes;
   - look at every figure and the project in screenshots, at the width a reader has;
   - the destination image: the project with its panel closed, the iframe clipped one pixel inside, at 2×.
9. **The student reader,** before the author: run the project's agent `student-reader`
   (`.claude/agents/student-reader.md`) on the chapter, after the ones before it. It reads as a student who programs
   a little: it does every exercise before opening the answer, follows the consigna, and reports where it got lost,
   with a fix for each, most serious first. Fix what it found that holds (it can be wrong: check each finding against
   the chapter), and note in the commit what was fixed.
10. **The author's reading:** the chapter is read start to end before the next one; what stumbles is fixed first.
11. **Commit** only the tutorial's paths, by name (the phase 5 session shares the folder), and push on the author's
    word.

## 4. What comes next

- **4. Girar y mover.** The loop arrives (erase, update, draw; the time between frames), turning by sine and cosine
  (where the axes go), shifting, scaling; the canvas's `rotate`, `translate`, `scale`. Breaks: moved then turned, it
  orbits; speed per frame runs faster on a faster screen. The project could set the sky in motion (the moon's arc,
  a turning windmill or the constellations' slow rotation round a pole star), so the night starts to move.
- **5. Matrices.** The 3×3 matrix with its shift (homogeneous coordinates), composing by multiplying, the inverse
  (what the mouse pressed on a turned piece), a hierarchy (a sun, an earth, a moon); the canvas's `setTransform`,
  `save`, `restore`.
- **6. Flechas.** Vectors in the plane and the dot product, proved three ways, with projection, rejection and the
  reflection: a ball among slanted walls; three.js's `Vector2` beside ours. The first prototype's derivations and
  its `shadow` figure (`site/src/lessons/material/`) are its material. It closes the plane.
- **7. Un cubo** opens space, and with it a design question to settle first: how the two ways look in 3D (our renderer
  on a 2D canvas beside three.js's WebGL view, both drawing one world as data, probably a tool in `herramientas/`).
  Its material: the first prototype's chapter 1 (`docs/tutorial-v3-material/`, `site/src/course/material/01-un-punto`)
  and the figures `parte-1/coordinates` and `parte-1/projection`.

## 5. Things learned, worth not relearning

- The canvas's numbers are Chrome's: its gradient dithers by one level, its edges are its own approximation (further
  from the true area than ours at 8×8 samples). The text says "in Chrome"; the tests check directions.
- `follow` watches the drawing, not the whole block, and only on the way in: one example runs at a time, the one in
  front of the reader.
- A step's files are read-only for tools by the `herramientas/` prefix (LiveCode and `course.ts` both look at it).
- The reader's `main.ts` holds only the two ways of painting (`paintOurs`, `paintTheirs`) and hands them to
  `comparePanel`; the panel, the counts and the star picking are the tool's. A chapter that needs more of the panel
  changes the tool, not `main.ts`. Every canvas call `paintTheirs` uses is explained in the text before the consigna.
- What the student reader caught in chapters 1–3, worth checking in every chapter before it runs: a claim that
  sounds right and is false ("closer to A, more weight"), a definition without sign used later with sign, a rule
  used before it is said (the rectangle's tie), a number in the text that is not the figure's (corners, not centres),
  an exercise whose answer the sentence before already gave, a question asked and never answered, code the consigna
  points at that the text never walked through, and TypeScript nobody said how to read.
- Pixel centres are at the halves; corners at whole numbers keep every edge sum exact.
- The browser tests centre the drawing (`reach`): the site's header covers a panel's top otherwise. The page's own
  column is `.sl-container > .sl-markdown-content` (margins and consignas open their own).
- CI's performance budget "the hash costs less than eight of Rapier's steps" once failed at 8.33 on CI's machine with
  nothing changed in the engine; a rerun passed.
- The two sessions: this one writes the tutorial, the phase 5 one the games, in one folder on main. Each commits its
  own paths only, and forwards to the other what belongs to it (a failing test, feedback).
