# Parte 4. La física: the lesson sheets

The design each lesson of part 4 is written from, under the approved [curriculum](../tutorial-curriculum.md) (§5, part 4) and [decision 0021](../decisions/0021-a-new-tutorial.md). Abbreviations and conventions are the curriculum's §2: **MF** (Millington's _Game Physics Engine Development_, the part's lead), **G2**, **E**, **L1**, **MA**, **GPP**. Every formula is in our conventions: SI units, column vectors, right-handed, +Y up, gravity a field, a fixed step _h_ = 1/60 s, semi-implicit Euler (velocity first, then position with the new velocity). MF writes quaternions [w x y z] and puts the contact normal on the contact frame's _x_ axis: translate both. Book text is never copied; chapter and section titles are cited.

**MF corrected in place.** The readers found errors in MF, and each lesson that leans on it corrects them where they occur (several become "corregí al autor" exercises): superposition called D'Alembert's principle (4.1); the projectile-scaling rule (4.1, 4.14); explicit and semi-implicit Euler switched without notice (4.2, 4.6); momentum conservation called "perfectly elastic" and impulse defined as a change of velocity (4.3); exact float equality in the rod (4.4); the centre of mass as an equal-mass cut (4.7); τ = **I**α without **ω** × **Iω**, the quaternion update stated without derivation, products of inertia explained by spinning tops, and code that disagrees with its equations (4.8, 4.2); a sleep measure that adds quantities of different units (4.12). MA's firing-solution code (4.14) is corrected too.

## The part sheet

**What the reader can do at the end.** Model forces, masses and force generators; derive why a stiff spring explodes under a fixed step, and the bound that says when; resolve collisions by impulses with restitution and friction; resolve many contacts at once and treat ropes and rods as contacts; build, play and hash a particle engine written by hand. Reason about rotation in the plane and in space: torque, the inertia tensor, angular momentum as the state, the gyroscopic term, the quaternion's derivative. Compute the impulse at a contact point from its effective mass, and generate a contact manifold. Drive Rapier through a small boundary in the right order around the step; diagnose and tune a simulation (solver iterations, continuous detection, sleep). Push and grab with mass. Aim a cannon with an exact firing solution. Play the tower game.

**The introduction page.** "¿Querés física en tu juego?" (G2 §14.1): physics gives interactive, emergent motion and costs control, predictability and tuning time; it needs determinism for replays and an authority for networked play; the design decides the physics, not the reverse. A "Sutileza" on bending the rules (G2: grenades with extra drag, game gravity of 15–20 m/s², MF). And MF's staircase drawn as a picture: particles → aggregates → rigid bodies, the first two built by hand and hashed, Rapier taking over where boxes rotate, stack and rest.

**The closing game: _voltear la torre_ (4.15).** A tower of boxes on a platform, its layout given as data (a pyramid, a column, a castle) with blocks of wood, stone and ice (4.5's materials). Part 1's turret is now the cannon (4.14). The player clicks a target point (3.5, 3.9); the cannon shows the firing solution's arc (0, 1 or 2 arcs; low or high chosen with a key) and fires a projectile from a pool, with continuous detection. Each round has _N_ shots (par from the layout), one explosive shell (a radial impulse) and a wind drawn from the seed, shown as an arrow. The tower is put to sleep at load, so it stands perfectly still until hit; asleep blocks are tinted. A block counts as fallen when its centre drops 0,5 m below the platform's top or leaves its footprint, checked when it sleeps or after 5 s. **Score:** 100 per fallen block + 250 per unused shot. **Win:** at least 80 % of the blocks off. **Lose:** the shots run out first. Restart replays the same seed, and 2.15's ghost shows the best round's shots. It exercises the firing solution, impulses, friction and restitution by material, stacking stability, sleep, the object pool, continuous detection, events through the queue (a block fell → score) and the hash.

## The lessons

### 4.1 Fuerzas y masa (C)

- **Question:** ¿Qué hace una fuerza con algo que tiene masa?
- **Before:** 2.2 (Euler steps, explicit and semi-implicit); 2.1 (state and step); 1.4 (vectors).
- **Leads:** MF ch. 3 (the particle, Newton's laws, inverse mass, damping, tuned gravity), §4.1 (ballistic presets), ch. 5 (the force accumulator; force generators). **Also:** G2 §14.4.1–14.4.3 (the rigid-body idealization, units, force as a function of time, position and velocity); MA §3.1.3 (actuation: a requested change of velocity becomes a force); Witkin and Baraff (particle dynamics).
- **Approach:** MF's particle and its force generators, a pattern: anything that pushes is a function of the state that adds a force. MF's vocabulary is corrected as it comes (superposition, not D'Alembert). **Hook:** 2.8's ball was moved by setting its velocity; 4.14's cannon must throw things whose paths depend on their mass and on the wind. That is a force, not a velocity.
- **The mathematics:** (1) The particle's state (**x**, **v**); **a** = **F**/_m_ with **F** = Σ**F**ᵢ (superposition: forces on one body add as vectors). (2) Inverse mass _w_ = 1/_m_: _w_ = 0 is immovable, and a massless particle has no expression (a feature). (3) Generators: gravity _m_**g** (so **a** = **g** whatever the mass: Galileo); drag −**v̂**(*k*₁|**v**| + *k*₂|**v**|²); wind as drag relative to the air, **v** − **v**wind. (4) Accumulate, integrate semi-implicitly, clear. (5) Terminal speed (closed forms MF does not give): *k*₁ alone, _v_ = _mg_/*k*₁; *k*₂ alone, _v_ = √(_mg_/*k*₂). (6) The projectile _x_(_t_) = *x*₀ + **v**₀*t* + ½**g** *t*²; its path _y_ = _x_ tan θ − *gx*²/(2*v*₀² cos² θ) depends only on _g_/*v*₀². **Correction:** to slow a shot by a factor _k_ and keep its path, _g_ scales by *k*², not by _k_ as MF writes. (7) A hit transfers momentum: a light fast ball changes a heavy crate's velocity by about (1 + _e_)_mv_/_M_ (forward to 4.3), not by its kinetic energy. **Worked example:** a 0,5 kg ball with *k*₂ = 0,01 kg/m: terminal speed √490,5 ≈ 22,1 m/s; with *k*₁ = 0,1 N·s/m alone, 49 m/s. A 30 m/s shot slowed to 15 m/s (_k_ = 2) keeps its arc with _g_ = 9,81/4 ≈ 2,45 m/s².
- **The live figure:** ballistics with MF's presets (a fast light pistol round, a heavy arcing artillery shell, a fireball that floats, a laser bolt with no gravity); sliders for _g_, *k*₁, *k*₂ and wind; the trail; the exact parabola drawn over the stepped flight. Toggle: "scale _g_ by _k_" against "by *k*²", each laid over the original path. Panel: **v**, |**v**|, terminal speed predicted and measured, range, apex. **Expected:** the measured terminal speed within 0,1 m/s of 22,1; the *k*² path on the original within 1 mm, the _k_ path visibly off.
- **Traps:** mass stored where inverse mass is expected (things fly at a touch, MF). A per-step drag reverses the motion when _kh_/_m_ > 1 (MF Ex. 5.2): 2.3's factor again. Game gravity is tuned (MF suggests 15–20 m/s²): bending the rules. Determinism: |**v**| needs √ (exactly rounded), but damping is a per-step constant written as a literal, never `Math.pow` (2.9's rule). Engines keep gravity as an acceleration (G2 §14.4.9.1); ours is a field.
- **Exercises:** (1) the same force for 1 s on _m_ and on 2*m*: the ratio of their speeds (2 : 1, MF Ex. 3.1). (2) "Corregí al autor": derive the *k*² rule from the path's equation. (3) An attraction to a point, then inverse-square: a small orbit (MF Ex. 5.3–5.4), the seed of 10.3.
- **Our angle:** force generators are how wind (4.14), explosions, magnets and strange gravity (10.3) enter. Manifold keeps gravity as a field stored as data (`{ kind: 'uniform', vector }`, `gravityAt` in `gravity.ts`) and nothing assumes −*y* (Bitácora 1a); every Manifold body has damping as data because Rapier has no rolling resistance and a ball rolled forever (Bitácora 1a).
- **Sources:** MF ch. 3, §4.1, ch. 5; G2 §14.4.1–14.4.3, §14.4.9.1; MA §3.1.3. Free: Witkin and Baraff, [_Physically Based Modeling_](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~baraff/sigcourse/); Glenn Fiedler, [_Integration Basics_](https://gafferongames.com/post/integration_basics/).
- **Reuses:** 1-00's "try the Moon's gravity" (−1,6 m/s²); nothing else.
- **Open:** whether the project's drag is linear or quadratic (quadratic needs |**v**|, which is allowed).

### 4.2 Resortes (C)

- **Question:** ¿Por qué un resorte duro explota, y qué dice eso de los choques?
- **Before:** 4.1; 2.3 (Euler's factor and its three regimes); 1.8 (matrices; the determinant as an area).
- **Leads:** MF ch. 6 (Hooke's law, anchored springs, bungees, buoyancy, stiff springs, the "fake implicit" spring). **Also:** G2 §14.4.4 (the properties of a method: convergence, order, stability); Catto (2009: why games use symplectic Euler); Holden (springs advanced exactly).
- **Approach:** the curriculum's main path: the oscillator in phase space, 2.3's question asked again in two dimensions. MF tells the story (a stiff spring explodes, so contacts are not forces); we prove the theorem MF leaves as an exercise. **Hook:** a "soft floor" made of a spring under the ball, the obvious way to do contacts (a penalty force): too soft and the ball sinks into it; stiffer, and at some point it explodes.
- **The mathematics:** (1) Hooke in 3D: **d** = **x**A − **x**B, **f**A = −*k* (|**d**| − ℓ₀) **d̂**, **f**B = −**f**A, damping −*c* ((**v**A − **v**B)·**d̂**) **d̂**. (2) _x_″ = −ω²*x* with ω = √(_k_/_m_): _x_(_t_) = *x*₀ cos ω*t* + (*v*₀/ω) sin ω*t* (try an exponential). With damping, the regimes by the sign of *c*² − 4ω² (per unit mass). (3) One step as a matrix acting on the state (_x_, _v_), written by rows: explicit **E** = (1, _h_ ; −ω²*h*, 1), det **E** = 1 + ω²*h*² > 1, so the area of a cloud of states grows every step and the energy with it; semi-implicit **S** = (1 − ω²*h*², _h_ ; −ω²*h*, 1), det **S** = 1 exactly: **S** is a kick (a shear) followed by a drift (a shear), each of determinant 1 (symplectic). (4) Eigenvalues of a 2×2 from its trace and determinant (λ² − (tr) λ + det = 0, taught here). For **S**, tr = 2 − ω²*h*²: the eigenvalues are complex conjugates on the unit circle iff ω*h* ≤ 2, and the motion stays bounded iff ω*h* < 2 (at ω*h* = 2 a double eigenvalue −1 with a Jordan block grows linearly). So _k_ < 4*m*/*h*². (5) Explicit Euler with per-step damping _D_: det = _D_ (1 + ω²*h*²), bounded in the oscillating regime iff _D_ (1 + ω²*h*²) < 1. (6) **What it says about collisions:** a penalty contact is a spring; hit at speed _v_ it sinks _A_ = _v_/ω, and stability forces ω < 2/_h_, so _A_ exceeds _vh_/2 whatever the mass: at 5 m/s and 60 Hz, more than 4,2 cm. A floor that looks rigid cannot be a stable spring; hence impulses (4.3). **Worked example:** _m_ = 1 kg, _h_ = 1/60 s: _k_ < 14 400 N/m; at _k_ = 3 600 N/m (ω*h* = 1) a ball arriving at 5 m/s sinks 8,3 cm.
- **The live figure:** (a) a mass on a spring at a fixed _h_, a stiffness slider, and the phase-space plot (_x_, _v_) with det and |λ| read out; explicit against semi-implicit (the toggle); one knob, two failures: spongy at low _k_ (sink depth measured), an explosion past ω*h* = 2 (energy growth measured). (b) The three damping regimes with the exact curve over the stepped one. (c) The penalty floor: a ball dropped on a spring floor, depth against _k_. Perturbation: the step slider (the bound moves as 4*m*/*h*²). Panel: ω, ω*h*, det **E**, det **S**, |λ|, energy after 10 s, sink depth. **Expected:** det **S** = 1,000 (to rounding) at every _k_; at ω*h* = 1,9 the energy stays within a few per cent; at 2,1 it grows by orders of magnitude in seconds; det **E** = 1 + ω²*h*².
- **Traps:** **correction:** MF's particle integrator is explicit Euler and its rigid-body one semi-implicit, never announced; say which is which, and why area decides. A spring force written with |ℓ − ℓ₀| loses its sign and only pulls (MF's rigid-body spring code); MF's buoyancy code has an operator-precedence slip: derive the formula, do not copy it. The exact ("fake implicit") spring needs a fixed anchor and nothing else pushing (MF's own caveat): right for a camera (5.5), wrong for a bridge. Determinism: the exact solution needs cos and exp, so it stays out of the hash (or uses our `trig.ts`).
- **Exercises:** (1) MF Ex. 6.5–6.6, closed: the largest stable _k_ for several dampings; derive it, then check it in the figure (hint: trace and determinant). (2) Buoyancy: a block of density 600 kg/m³ floats with what fraction under water? (0,6). (3) Prove det **S** = 1 by writing **S** as the product of the kick and the drift.
- **Our angle:** the phase-space determinant is 1.8's determinant as an area, put to work; Rapier integrates semi-implicitly, and Manifold through it. The spring advanced exactly is 5.5's follow camera (Holden); "stiffness against the step" is a lab-kit figure of its own. Manifold has no springs of its own; 4.13's grab is a damped, capped spring written as a velocity change.
- **Sources:** MF ch. 6; G2 §14.4.3–14.4.4. Free: Erin Catto, [_Numerical Integration_](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_NumericalIntegration_GDC2009.pdf) (GDC 2009); Daniel Holden, [_Spring-It-On_](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call); Hairer, Lubich and Wanner (for the author).
- **Reuses:** 0-03's fixed-step figure, only as 2.2 and 2.4 recall it.
- **Open:** eigenvalues have not been taught before (see the problems); the curriculum's "ω*h* ≤ 2" is right for the circle, while boundedness needs the strict inequality.

### 4.3 Impulsos y choques (C)

- **Question:** ¿Cómo cambia una velocidad de golpe cuando dos cosas chocan?
- **Before:** 4.2 (why not a spring); 3.7 (the contact normal from the closest point); 3.14 (the slide).
- **Leads:** MF §7.1–7.2.2 (separating velocity, restitution, impulse, interpenetration), corrected; G2 §14.4.7.1–4 (an ideal impulse under Newton's law of restitution; energy; the reflection check; penalty forces as the alternative). **Also:** E §5.2.1 (the penetration depth is the shortest separating translation); Catto (2006: sequential impulses).
- **Approach:** a collision is too fast and too stiff to simulate (4.2 proved it), so jump straight to the velocities after it: two equations (momentum, restitution), two unknowns. G2's derivation of the linear case, MF's names and failure stories, our corrections. **Hook:** 3.14's slide is a contact with _e_ = 0; balls that bounce off each other need an _e_ and two masses.
- **The mathematics:** (1) Separating velocity *v*s = (**v**a − **v**b)·**n** (**n** from _b_ toward _a_); closing if *v*s < 0, and only then resolved. (2) Newton's law of restitution: *v*s′ = −*e* *v*s, with 0 ≤ _e_ ≤ 1. (3) An impulse is a change of momentum, **J** = Δ(_m_ **v**) = ∫**F** d*t* (N·s), here _j_ **n**: Δ**v**a = _j_ **n**/*m*a and Δ**v**b = −*j* **n**/*m*b; solving, _j_ = −(1 + _e_) *v*s/(*w*a + *w*b), with _w_ the inverse masses. Momentum is conserved for every _e_, by construction (equal and opposite impulses); kinetic energy drops by ½ μ (1 − *e*²) *v*s², with μ = *m*a *m*b/(*m*a + *m*b), and is conserved only at _e_ = 1. (4) Checks: *m*b → ∞ with _e_ = 1 gives **v**′ = **v** − 2(**v**·**n**) **n**, 3.4's reflection; _e_ = 0 gives 3.14's slide. "El mismo movimiento": restitution acts on the normal part only. (5) Interpenetration: move apart by the depth, shared in proportion to the inverse masses (*m*a Δa = *m*b Δb), which keeps the centre of mass in place (the reading MF does not give). (6) Bounce height: _e_ = √(_h_′/_h_), from _v_ = √(2*gh*). **Worked example** (G2's): 1 kg at +2 m/s against 3 kg at −2 m/s, _e_ = 0,5: *v*s = −4 m/s, _j_ = 4,5 N·s, velocities after −2,5 and −0,5 m/s; momentum −4 kg·m/s before and after; energy 8 J → 3,5 J (½ · 0,75 · 0,75 · 16 = 4,5 J lost).
- **The live figure:** two balls on a line with sliders for the masses and _e_; momentum and energy bars before and after; a ball on a half-space with an _e_ slider (the reflection at _e_ = 1); MF's box on a planet, where equal shares of the depth would move the planet. Toggle: "push each apart by half the depth" against shares by inverse mass. Panel: *v*s, _j_, velocities after, momentum and energy before and after, the centre of mass before and after the separation. **Expected:** the numbers above; momentum equal to 10⁻¹²; the centre of mass unmoved.
- **Traps:** **corrections:** MF says conservation of momentum defines a "perfectly elastic" collision; momentum is conserved in every collision, and perfectly elastic means _e_ = 1. MF defines impulse as a change of velocity; it is a change of momentum, in N·s. Resolve closing contacts only: a deep pair may already be separating (no impulse, still overlapping; the position pass handles it). Very fast pairs pass through each other between steps (3.15).
- **Exercises:** (1) MF Ex. 7.3: two stacked balls dropped from 1 m, the bottom one 9 times heavier, _e_ = 0,5 with the floor and 0,75 between them: the top ball leaves at about 6,0 m/s and rises about 1,86 m (hint: the floor's bounce first, then the pair). (2) Derive _e_ = √(_h_′/_h_) (MF Ex. 7.2). (3) "Corregí al autor": show that momentum is conserved in a collision with _e_ = 0.
- **Our angle:** "el mismo movimiento" (§6.2: restitution on the normal part). The normal impulse is the natural "how hard" of an impact sound; Manifold derives impacts from the velocities before and after the step instead of Rapier's events, which are not part of the world's state (Bitácora 3f). _j_ uses + − × ÷ only.
- **Sources:** MF §7.1–7.2.2, Ex. 7.1–7.4; G2 §14.4.7.1–4; E §5.2.1. Free: [Catto's publications](https://box2d.org/publications/) (Sequential Impulses, 2006); Randy Gaul, [_How to Create a Custom 2D Physics Engine_](https://code.tutsplus.com/how-to-create-a-custom-2d-physics-engine-the-basics-and-impulse-resolution--gamedev-6331t); Chris Hecker, [_Rigid Body Dynamics_](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://www.chrishecker.com/Rigid_Body_Dynamics).
- **Reuses:** 1-01's exercise 3 (a tiny solver that swaps normal velocities for equal masses) as the warm-up.
- **Open:** nothing.

### 4.4 Muchos contactos a la vez: reposo, cuerdas y varillas (C)

- **Question:** ¿Cómo se queda quieto algo apoyado, y qué es una cuerda para la física?
- **Before:** 4.3.
- **Leads:** MF §7.2.3–7.5 (resting contacts, the iterative resolver, cables and rods), ch. 8 (the mass-aggregate engine: bridges and platforms). **Also:** G2 §14.4.8, §14.4.10 (the catalogue of constraints; collisions as constraints; the step's iterations); Jakobsen (2001: constraints by projection).
- **Approach:** MF's "every connection is a contact": collisions, resting contacts, cables and rods share one data shape. Our framing on top: a constraint is a condition on positions whose derivative is a condition on velocities, which generalizes to Rapier's joints (5.10). **Hook:** a ball resting on the floor with _e_ = 0,5 buzzes: each step gravity adds a velocity into the floor and the impulse bounces it back; a row of balls: fixing one contact breaks the next.
- **The mathematics:** (1) Resting contact: in one step gravity adds _gh_ of velocity along −**n**; remove from the closing velocity what one step of acceleration added (normal direction only, so sliding is untouched), and set _e_ = 0 below a small closing speed. (2) Many contacts: resolve the worst (most negative *v*s) first, recompute the ones it touched, repeat up to a cap (about twice the number of contacts), ties broken by contact index. Positions in a second pass, deepest first, updating depths by how far bodies moved. It is Gauss–Seidel on velocities (named here; the accumulated version is 4.12's). (3) Constraints: _C_ = |**x**a − **x**b| − _L_. A cable is _C_ ≤ 0 (a contact only when stretched, pulling together); a rod is _C_ = 0 (always a contact, its normal by the sign of _C_, depth |_C_|, _e_ = 0); a contact is distance ≥ 0. Differentiating, _Ċ_ = (**v**a − **v**b)·**n**: impulses enforce the velocity condition, projection the position condition. Inequality against equality is the whole difference. (4) The step: forces → integrate → generate contacts → resolve velocities → resolve positions. **Worked examples:** at 60 Hz gravity adds 0,1635 m/s per step; with _e_ = 0,5 a naive resolver throws the ball up at 0,082 m/s every step, a buzz of about 0,3 mm; with the fix it rests exactly. Three equal balls in a row, the first arriving with _e_ = 1: worst-first passes the velocity to the last ball in 2 iterations.
- **The live figure:** a resting particle with and without the fix (the trace of _y_; the toggle); a row of three balls resolved contact by contact with an iteration counter and a "one iteration" button; a pendulum on a rod and on a cable (thrown up, the cable goes slack); a rope bridge (particles, rods along it, cables up to anchors) and MF's tilting platform on infinite-mass pivots (_w_ = 0). Perturbation: a huge impulse on the bridge. One knob, two failures: the iteration cap (too few: the bridge stretches; too many: the cost). Panel: iterations used, largest |_C_|, largest penetration, resting buzz. **Expected:** buzz 0,000 with the fix; the row in 2 iterations; the bridge's stretch falls as the cap rises.
- **Traps:** **correction:** MF's rod tests the length for exact float equality with its rest length; a rod always generates its contact (normal by the sign of _C_, depth |_C_|) and needs no equality test. A rod that generates only one of its two contacts in a step vibrates (MF). An iteration cap hides unresolved contacts: debug with none. Time-division engines (stop at each exact collision time) loop forever on resting contacts, fine for billiards only (MF §7.3.2). Determinism: ties by index; the order is part of the result.
- **Exercises:** (1) a "loose rod" with contacts only outside [*L*min, *L*max] (MF Ex. 7.6). (2) A cube of eight particles: how many rods make it rigid? (18: the 12 edges and one diagonal per face; MF Mini-project 8.2). (3) Throw the rod pendulum and the cable pendulum upward and explain the difference with _C_ = 0 against _C_ ≤ 0.
- **Our angle:** "a constraint is a contact you always generate" (MF) leads to Rapier's joints (5.10); ties broken by index is the resolver's rule in the determinism thread (§6.1). Manifold resolves nothing of its own (Rapier's solver), and its `touching(handle)` lists the bodies in contact, in order.
- **Sources:** MF §7.2.3–7.5, ch. 8; G2 §14.4.8, §14.4.10. Free: Thomas Jakobsen, [_Advanced Character Physics_](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/academic/class/15462-s13/www/lec_slides/Jakobsen.pdf); Müller, [_Ten Minute Physics_](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/tenMinutePhysics/index.html).
- **Reuses:** nothing.
- **Open:** nothing.

### 4.5 Rozamiento y rebote (C)

- **Question:** ¿Qué decide si algo resbala, se frena o rebota?
- **Before:** 4.4.
- **Leads:** MF §15.2–15.4 (microcollisions; Coulomb; friction as a clamped impulse), §16.1.2 (creeping on slopes), App. B (coefficients). **Also:** G2 §14.4.7.5 (the incline: _mg_(sin θ − μ cos θ)).
- **Approach:** Coulomb's model with one coefficient (the named assumption 0021 asks for), first as G2's incline, then as MF's clamp on an impulse; the cone as the set of allowed contact impulses and the clamp as a projection onto it. Particle friction (MF: the easy case); the rigid version waits for 4.9. **Hook:** the ball slides forever on stone; on a ramp a box should rest or slide by its material; rubber should bounce and clay should not.
- **The mathematics:** (1) Static friction: |**f**t| ≤ μs _N_, equal to what is needed below the limit; dynamic: |**f**t| = μd _N_ against the sliding velocity; independent of the contact area; games merge them (Rapier: one μ). (2) The incline: weight split into _mg_ cos θ and _mg_ sin θ (projection and rejection, 1.5); it rests iff tan θ ≤ μs; otherwise it accelerates at _g_ (sin θ − μ cos θ). (3) As impulses: each step a normal impulse *j*n (for a resting particle about _mgh_ cos θ); the tangential impulse that would stop the sliding, *j*t\* = −*m* **v**t; if |*j*t\*| ≤ μ *j*n it is applied (stick), else scaled to length μ *j*n (slip). The cone {*j*n ≥ 0, |*j*t| ≤ μ *j*n}; the clamp is a radial projection (not the orthogonal one: an aside for the mathematician; Rapier clamps this way too). (4) Bounce: restitution from the materials, set to 0 below a small closing speed (microcollisions). (5) Two materials, one number per contact: the average (Rapier's default), the minimum, the product or the maximum: a design rule, not physics. **Worked example:** an incline at 25°, tan 25° = 0,466: ice (μ = 0,33) slides at 9,81 (0,4226 − 0,2991) ≈ 1,21 m/s²; glass (0,45) at 0,145 m/s²; stone (0,6) holds. μ = 0,4 starts sliding at arctan 0,4 = 21,8°.
- **The live figure:** a block under a growing push: friction follows the push, then drops when it slips (separate μs and μd as an option); an incline whose angle you raise; the cone with the impulse arrow being projected; balls of several materials dropped from 2 m, bouncing to *e*² · 2 m. Toggle: friction as a constant deceleration that overshoots zero (the block jitters at rest) against the clamp (it stops exactly). Panel: θ, tan θ, μ, stick or slip, acceleration, bounce height against *e*²*h*. **Expected:** 1,21 and 0,145 m/s² (to 0,01); bounce = *e*² · 2 m on the hand-built engine (4.11 compares Rapier's).
- **Traps:** an impulse engine has no normal force, only a normal impulse per step, and the clamp uses it; merging μs and μd loses stick–slip; the velocity gravity builds along a slope in one step makes resting blocks creep downhill unless that is removed too (MF §16.1.2, back in 4.12); combine rules surprise (with the minimum, ice slides on anything; with the maximum, rubber holds on ice). Determinism: |**v**t| needs √, which is allowed.
- **Exercises:** (1) the angle at which each box starts to slide under the average rule (stone on stone, 0,6: 31,0°). (2) MF Ex. 15.3 at home: μs from a box, a string and coins over a table's edge (μs = hanging mass / box mass at the slip). (3) Prove that the clamp keeps the impulse in the cone and never reverses the sliding direction.
- **Our angle:** materials are data with names (Manifold: `MATERIALS` and `tractionOf` in `materials.ts`; a level declares its own in one line; one coefficient and the average, as Rapier). "El mismo movimiento": friction on the tangential part. Measured in Manifold: a box just past its friction angle falls asleep in Rapier and stays there forever; the hand-built engine has no sleep, so here it slides: 4.12 compares the two.
- **Sources:** MF §15.2–15.4, §16.1.2, App. B; G2 §14.4.7.5. Free: [Rapier's colliders](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/colliders) (friction, restitution, combine rules); Allen Chou, [_Contact Constraints_](https://allenchou.net/2013/12/game-physics-resolution-contact-constraints/).
- **Reuses:** 3-05 (fricción y rebote): the 25° incline with ice, glass and stone, tan θ, the *e*² bounce, the four combine rules with their numbers, materials as data, its exercises. Its Rapier findings (a 0,484 m bounce instead of 0,5, the box that sleeps) move to 4.11–4.12.

### 4.6 Un motor de juguete (P)

- **Question:** ¿Puede el proyecto tener una física escrita por nosotros y entendida entera?
- **Before:** 4.5 (everything a particle can do); 3.14 (`collide.ts`, `Grid`, the walls); 2.14 and 2.15 (replays, `hash.ts`).

The billiard numbers: balls of radius 0,057 m and 0,17 kg, _e_ = 0,95 between them, rolling drag on the cloth.

### 4.7 Girar en el plano (C) ◇

- **Question:** ¿Qué hace girar algo, y cuánto le cuesta?
- **Before:** 4.1 (force and mass); 1.6 (the cross product; the 2D signed area); 4.6 (the particle engine).
- **Leads:** G2 §14.4.5 (2D first: angle, angular speed, moment of inertia, torque as the _z_ part of **r** × **F**; every linear quantity has a twin); MF §9.1.3 (the centre of mass and the choice of origin), §10.1–10.3 (torque; accumulating torques; couples). **Also:** MF ch. 18 (rotation in 2D as a unit complex number).
- **Approach:** G2's "2D first", MF's centre of mass and couples. The two-column dictionary starts here (linear | angular): position | angle, velocity | ω, mass | _I_, force | τ, momentum | _L_. **Hook:** the toy engine's particles never turn; a board hit off its centre must move and spin at once.
- **The mathematics:** (1) The centre of mass _c_ = Σ*m*ᵢ**x**ᵢ/_M_, the point where the first moment vanishes; the motion splits there into a particle motion of _c_ and a rotation about it. **Correction:** MF says any line through it cuts the body into equal masses; false: a uniform triangle cut through its centroid parallel to a side splits 4/9 and 5/9. (2) 2D rotation: θ, ω = θ̇; a point's velocity **v**c + ω × **r** (in 2D, ω(−*r*ᵧ, *r*ₓ)). (3) Torque τ = **r** × **F** (its _z_ part, 1.6's signed area), _I_ = Σ*m*ᵢ|**r**ᵢ|², α = τ/_I_. (4) A force through _c_ gives no torque; two forces that cancel can still turn (a couple), so torques are accumulated per force, never from the net force. (5) The orientation as a unit complex number (cos θ, sin θ): the circle, the first manifold of rotations; integrating θ by adding works in 2D and fails in 3D (4.8). **Worked example:** a board 2 m × 0,2 m of 3 kg on ice, _I_ = _m_(*L*² + *w*²)/12 ≈ 1,01 kg·m²; 6 N pushed perpendicular at one end: _a_ = 2 m/s², τ = 6 N·m, α ≈ 5,94 rad/s² (6 with the thin-rod _I_ = 1). The same push at the centre: α = 0. A couple of 6 N at both ends: _a_ = 0, α ≈ 11,9 rad/s².
- **The live figure:** a 2D board on ice with a force arrow dragged around its edge: the centre's acceleration does not change, the spin does; the couple ("a knob"); a point-mass editor (drag masses and watch the centre move; for an L or a C it falls outside the body, G2); **v** + ω × **r** drawn at the corners; the dictionary as a live widget. Toggle: torque from the net force (zero for a couple) against per-force accumulation. Panel: _c_, _M_, _I_, **F**net, τ, _a_, α. **Expected:** the numbers above; the triangle cut 0,444 / 0,556.
- **Traps:** **correction** of MF's centre of mass; a force's direction in world space with its point in body space (MF's convenience that bites); angular velocity is not the derivative of an "orientation vector", despite MF's θ̇ (in 3D it is the derivative of nothing); adding to θ is only valid in 2D.
- **Exercises:** (1) MF Ex. 10.1 and 10.3: the total force and torque from forces at given points. (2) The centre of mass of an L made of two rectangles, and a check that it lies outside (G2). (3) MF Ex. 9.7 at home: find a box's centre of mass by balancing it on a pencil along three axes.
- **Our angle:** the circle is the first rotation manifold, 1.15's 3-sphere's little brother; the dictionary grows through 4.9 (§6.6). Determinism: advance the unit complex number by a step and renormalize (√ only) rather than taking cos and sin of a growing angle (2.9's rule). In Manifold everything that rotates is Rapier's: the Dojo's spinning cube moved to Rapier because the engine's own `Math.sin` and `Math.cos` were not guaranteed equal across browsers (Bitácora 1a).
- **Sources:** G2 §14.4.5, §14.4.9.2–4; MF §9.1.3, §10.1–10.3, ch. 18. Free: [Hecker, part 1](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://www.chrishecker.com/Rigid_Body_Dynamics); Witkin and Baraff, [notes D1](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~baraff/sigcourse/notesd1.pdf).
- **Reuses:** nothing.
- **Open:** if folded, 4.8 opens with this board figure and the dictionary's first rows.

### 4.8 La inercia en el espacio (C)

- **Question:** ¿Por qué un libro que gira en el aire da vueltas raras?
- **Before:** 4.7; 1.11 (frames; **R**⁻¹ = **R**ᵀ from 1.9); 1.15 (quaternions, the 3-sphere, renormalizing).
- **Leads:** G2 §14.4.6 (the inertia tensor; ω is not conserved, angular momentum is; momentum as the state; q̇ = ½ω*q* stated); MF §10.2 (moments of inertia, the tensor, its world form), App. A (tensors of common solids). **Also:** L1 2.1.3 (transforming a transform), 2.7 (quaternions); Witkin and Baraff (rigid body dynamics).
- **Approach:** G2's order (angular momentum is the conserved state; ω is derived from it), with the derivations neither book gives: q̇ = ½ω*q* from 1.15, the gyroscopic term from **L** = **I**w**ω** with **I**w changing, principal axes from the spectral theorem. **Hook:** toss a book (or a phone) spinning about its middle axis and it flips; an engine that integrates ω like a velocity keeps it spinning smoothly.
- **The mathematics:** (1) The moment about an axis Σ*m*ᵢ*d*ᵢ²; the inertia tensor **I** = Σ*m*ᵢ(|**r**ᵢ|²**E** − **r**ᵢ**r**ᵢᵀ), symmetric. (2) The spectral theorem (stated; proof in "Más a fondo"): there are orthonormal principal axes in which **I** is diagonal, so products of inertia are an artefact of the axes chosen (correcting MF's spinning-top story). A box of sides _a_, _b_, _c_: **I** = (_m_/12) diag(*b*² + *c*², *a*² + *c*², *a*² + *b*²). (3) The world tensor **I**w = **R I R**ᵀ: a matrix carried to the world (the table's row, 1.11). (4) **L** = **I**w**ω** and d**L**/d*t* = **τ**; since **I**w changes, **τ** = **I**w**ω̇** + **ω** × **I**w**ω**. **Correction:** MF's τ = **I**α drops the second term, and its integrator does not conserve angular momentum. Integrate **L**, then **ω** = **R I**⁻¹**R**ᵀ**L**. (5) q̇ = ½**ω**_q_: for a constant **ω**, _q_(_t_) = _r_(_t_)*q*₀ with _r_(_t_) = (cos(|**ω**|_t_/2), sin(|**ω**|_t_/2)**ω̂**); its derivative at 0 is (0, **ω**/2)*q*₀ (MF and G2 state it). The step _q_ ← normalize(_q_ + (_h_/2)**ω**_q_) leaves the 3-sphere and projects back (1.15). (6) The middle axis is unstable: linearizing Euler's equations about it gives growth at the rate Ω√((*I*₃ − *I*₂)(*I*₂ − *I*₁)/(*I*₁*I*₃)) ("Más a fondo"; the main path is the figure). (7) The parallel-axis theorem **I** = **I**c + _m_(|**d**|²**E** − **dd**ᵀ), for composite bodies. **Worked example:** a box of 3 × 2 × 1 m and 6 kg: **I** = diag(2,5; 5; 6,5) kg·m². Spun at 2 rad/s about the 2 m axis (the middle one) with a 0,01 rad/s wobble, the growth rate is 0,96 s⁻¹ and the first flip comes after about 5 s; about the 1 m axis (6,5) it stays.
- **The live figure:** a box in zero gravity spun about each principal axis, with a wobble slider; integrated three ways side by side: ω as the state (naive, MF's), **L** as the state with the gyroscopic term (correct), and a Rapier body (measured); the inertia ellipsoid and the **L** vector drawn. Panel: **I**, **ω**, **L**, |**L**| drift over 10 s, energy drift, time to the first flip. **Expected:** with **L** as the state, |**L**| constant to 10⁻⁶ (relative) and the first flip near 5 s; with ω as the state, no flip; Rapier: to measure.
- **Traps:** **corrections:** τ = **I**α without **ω** × **Iω** (MF); the quaternion update stated, never derived (MF, G2); MF's code for "rotate by a vector" does not match its own equation (it multiplies on the other side and drops the ½). The tensor lives in the body and must be carried to the world every step. Tables of tensors may be about a base or an apex, not the centre of mass (MF App. A). Determinism: the Euler step with renormalization uses + − × ÷ √ only; the exact update _r_(_h_)_q_ needs sin and cos.
- **Exercises:** (1) MF Ex. 10.2: eight unit masses at a cube's corners: every product of inertia vanishes. (2) A T made of two boxes: the parallel-axis theorem by hand, then a check in the figure. (3) Derive q̇ = ½*q* **ω**body for an angular velocity given in the body's frame.
- **Our angle:** renormalizing is projecting back onto 1.15's 3-sphere (a strange-worlds seed). A measured finding for the "engine built in public" thread: does Rapier 0.21's deterministic build integrate the gyroscopic term? The figure's Rapier column answers it (MF's reader could not say), and since all of Manifold's bodies are Rapier's, the answer is Manifold's too.
- **Sources:** G2 §14.4.6; MF §10.2, App. A; L1 2.1.3, 2.7. Free: Witkin and Baraff, [notes D1](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~baraff/sigcourse/notesd1.pdf); Glenn Fiedler, [_Physics in 3D_](https://gafferongames.com/post/physics_in_3d/); David Eberly, [_Polyhedral Mass Properties_](https://www.geometrictools.com/Documentation/PolyhedralMassProperties.pdf) (for meshes).
- **Reuses:** nothing.
- **Open:** the spectral theorem has not been taught (see the problems); measure Rapier's gyroscopic behaviour before writing "En Manifold".

### 4.9 Choques que hacen girar (C)

- **Question:** ¿Cómo hace girar un choque que no pega en el centro?
- **Before:** 4.8 (the inverse inertia in the world); 4.3 (an impulse along a normal); 4.5 (the friction cone); 1.6 (a frame from one normal); 1.8 (the skew matrix [**a**]×).
- **Leads:** MF ch. 14 (an impulse at a point; the contact frame; the velocity change per unit impulse; nonlinear projection), §15.4 (friction with the 3×3 matrix); L1 1.5.2 (triple products, the skew matrix); Catto (effective mass, sequential impulses).
- **Approach:** MF's three-rulers story (the same contact and closing speed, three different responses), solved exactly; 4.3's derivation repeated with the contact point's velocity: the dictionary's next row (impulse | impulsive torque, mass | effective mass). **Hook:** in the toy engine a box (were there one) would land on a corner and bounce like a ball; real things spin.
- **The mathematics:** (1) An impulse **j** at **r** from the centre of mass: Δ**v** = **j**/_m_, Δ**ω** = **I**w⁻¹(**r** × **j**); the contact point moves at **u** = **v** + **ω** × **r**. (2) Along **n**: Δ*u*n = _jK_ with _K_ = *w*a + *w*b + (**r**a × **n**)·**I**a⁻¹(**r**a × **n**) + (**r**b × **n**)·**I**b⁻¹(**r**b × **n**). MF computes the angular term as **n**·((**I**⁻¹(**r** × **n**)) × **r**), the same number by the cyclic triple product (1.6). _K_ > 0 because **I**⁻¹ is symmetric positive definite, so _j_ = −(1 + _e_)*u*n/_K_ always exists (MF never remarks it); 1/_K_ is the effective mass at that point in that direction. (3) The contact frame: **n** and two tangents from a helper axis chosen by **n**'s smallest component (1.6, robust). (4) Friction: Δ**u** = **K j** with the 3×3 **K** = (*w*a + *w*b)**E** − [**r**a]×**I**a⁻¹[**r**a]× − [**r**b]×**I**b⁻¹[**r**b]×; solve for the **j** that stops sliding, then clamp to the cone (4.5). (5) "Más a fondo": MF's nonlinear projection (share the depth between translation and rotation in _K_'s proportions, with a cap) against Rapier's correction inside its solver. **Worked example (2D, _e_ = 0):** a bar 2 m long, 1 kg, falling flat at 3 m/s, one end lands (**r** = (1, 0), **n** = (0, 1)). As a rod, _I_ = 1/3: _K_ = 1 + 3 = 4, _j_ = 0,75 N·s, the centre still falls at 2,25 m/s, ω = 2,25 rad/s counterclockwise, and the contact point stops. A point mass: _K_ = 1, _j_ = 3, it stops. A dumbbell (mass at the ends, _I_ = 1): _K_ = 2, _j_ = 1,5, the centre at 1,5 m/s, ω = 1,5 rad/s.
- **The live figure:** the three rulers, with sliders for the mass distribution (point → rod → dumbbell) and the contact's offset; _K_'s terms as bars; the dictionary's row; a 2D box landing on a corner, computed by a 30-line routine whose source is shown; the friction cone in the contact frame, the 3×3 solve, then the clamp. Toggle: the impulse applied at the centre (no spin) against at the contact point. Panel: _K_'s terms, _j_, the centre's velocity after, ω after, the contact point's velocity after. **Expected:** 4 / 0,75 / −2,25 / 2,25 / 0,000; 1 / 3; 2 / 1,5.
- **Traps:** the second body receives −**j**; swapping the bodies flips **n**; the contact point inside an overlap is somewhat arbitrary (MF); "impulse" names both the linear impulse and the impulsive torque; the angular term needs the world inverse inertia (4.8).
- **Exercises:** (1) MF Ex. 14.4: two bars, mass at the ends against in the middle; one end hits the floor: whose centre keeps falling faster? (the rod's, 2,25 against 1,5 m/s). (2) Prove that _K_'s angular term equals (**r** × **n**)·**I**⁻¹(**r** × **n**) and is positive. (3) MF Ex. 14.5: a flat box with one corner sunk: the largest rotation before another corner sinks (why engines cap rotation).
- **Our angle:** the contact frame is 1.6's basis from one vector, and friction lives in its tangent plane ("el mismo movimiento"); _K_ generalizes to **J M**⁻¹**J**ᵀ, the matrix a solver works with (4.12's "Más a fondo"). Rapier computes all of this per contact inside `world.step()`; the lesson is what it computes. Manifold: nothing of its own.
- **Sources:** MF ch. 14, §15.4; L1 1.5.2. Free: [Catto's publications](https://box2d.org/publications/) (Sequential Impulses, 2006; Understanding Constraints, 2014); [Hecker, part 3](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://www.chrishecker.com/Rigid_Body_Dynamics); Witkin and Baraff, [notes D2](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~baraff/sigcourse/notesd2.pdf).
- **Reuses:** nothing.
- **Open:** nothing.

### 4.10 Del choque al contacto: puntos, normal, profundidad (C) ◇

- **Question:** ¿Dónde se tocan dos cajas, y por qué un punto no alcanza?
- **Before:** 4.9 (an impulse at a point); 3.10 (the separating axis, least overlap); 3.4 (planes); 3.11 (Minkowski, for the "Más a fondo").
- **Leads:** MF ch. 13 (contact data; feature pairs; sphere, half-space and box contacts; the separating axis; coherence); L2 §9.1 (polygon clipping with a thick plane); E §9.5 (GJK); Gregorius (2015: robust contact creation).
- **Approach:** MF's distinction: collision detection is not contact generation. A yes-or-no answer is not enough: the solver needs points, a normal and depths, and a box resting on a box needs several points or it rocks. L2's clipping builds the manifold (first needed here, as the curriculum decides). GJK is a "Más a fondo" (E's optional lesson): what the engine does for general convex pairs. **Hook:** a box on a box with only its deepest point as the contact rocks back and forth under 4.9's resolver (MF fig. 13.2).
- **The mathematics:** (1) Contact data: point, normal (from the first body toward the second), depth, and the materials' numbers (MF §13.2.2). (2) Feature pairs: vertex–face and edge–edge; face–face and edge–face reduce to them; vertex–vertex is ignored, because the next step turns it into a better case. (3) Box–half-space: test the eight corners; each with _s_ < 0 is a contact of depth −*s* (1, 2 or 4 when resting). (4) Box–sphere: the centre into the box's frame, clamp (3.7), back. (5) Box–box: the least-overlap axis (3.10). A face axis gives a reference face and an incident face (the other box's face most anti-parallel to it); clip the incident face against the reference face's side planes (Sutherland and Hodgman, with a thick plane |_s_| ≤ ε, L2 §9.1), keep the points below the reference face, depth −*s*. An edge axis gives one contact from 3.7's two-segment closest points. (6) Coherence: keep last step's feature pairs (MF §13.5), or ask the detector for a whole manifold. **Worked examples:** a unit cube resting flat: 4 contacts at its bottom corners; tilted 10° about an edge: 2; tilted about a diagonal: 1. A unit box on another, shifted 0,4 m in _x_: clipping gives four points, at _x_ = −0,1 and 0,5, _z_ = ±0,5 (an overlap of 0,6 × 1 m).
- **The live figure:** a box over a plane: tilt it and watch 1, 2 or 4 contacts appear with depth arrows; box–sphere with the clamped point; box–box with the reference and incident faces and the clipping animated edge by edge (a step button). Toggle: one deepest point (the box rocks under 4.9's resolver) against the clipped manifold (it rests). "Más a fondo": GJK in 2D on _A_ ⊖ _B_, one iteration per press (E Fig. 9.11). Panel: number of contacts, each depth, the normal, the rocking amplitude. **Expected:** 4 / 2 / 1; the shifted box's points as above; rocking above 0 with one point, about 0 with four.
- **Traps:** clipping without a thick plane emits duplicate or flickering points; the incident face must be the most anti-parallel one; manifolds flicker between steps without coherence; GJK on curved shapes converges only in the limit and needs a tolerance (E); EPA, which finds the depth after GJK, is not in E: name it only.
- **Exercises:** (1) the most contacts between a cube and a cylinder lying on its side (2: a line of contact clipped to the face; MF's exercise). (2) Clip a triangle against a thick plane by hand (L2 Fig. 9.1). (3) Run GJK by hand on a triangle and the origin ("Más a fondo").
- **Our angle:** se reduce a the separating axis (3.10) + clipping (here) + closest points (3.7); clipping returns for portals and decals (8.14, 8.16). Manifold's Dojo draws Rapier's contact points and normals with `show.contacts on` (the engine's `contacts()`): this figure at full size.
- **Sources:** MF ch. 13; L2 §9.1; E §9.5. ### 4.11 Un motor de física (P)

- **Question:** ¿Qué hace Rapier de lo que ya construimos, y cómo se le habla?
- **Before:** 4.6 (the toy engine); 4.10 (contacts); 4.8–4.9 (what rigid bodies need).

Coins and the exit become sensors, filtered by collision groups. The toy engine stays for effects (sparks, dust), stepped after Rapier. A collider created between two steps is invisible to queries until the next one (2-05). Events describe a world that may have changed (GPP): capture ids and handle them at a fixed phase; Rapier's events are not in a snapshot. ### 4.12 Cuando la física falla: estabilidad y sueño (C)

- **Question:** ¿Por qué una pila tiembla o se hunde, y qué perillas lo arreglan?
- **Before:** 4.11.
- **Leads:** MF ch. 16 (drift, creep, pessimistic contacts, scale, tolerances, sleep, islands), §15.5 (sequential resolution's artefacts), §17.3 (honest limits); G2 §14.3.5.7 (continuous detection), §14.4.7.7, §14.4.10 (coming to rest; the step's iterations). **Also:** Catto (islands, Solver2D, continuous collision).
- **Approach:** MF's catalogue of failures, each with its cause and cure, measured on Rapier with toggles (curriculum), and the solver explained honestly, which MF lacks: sequential impulses (projected Gauss–Seidel) with accumulated, clamped impulses and warm starting. **Hook:** a stack of ten crates trembles and sinks at the default settings; a cannonball goes through a wall; a box on a slope creeps.
- **The mathematics:** (1) Accumulated impulses: each contact keeps its total λ; an iteration computes Δλ and clamps the **total** (λn ≥ 0, |λt| ≤ μλn), so a later iteration can give back an earlier excess, which 4.4's worst-first resolver cannot; warm starting begins from last step's λ. Convergence slows with mass ratios and long chains. (2) Tunnelling: _vh_ > 2*r* + _w_ (3.15), and what continuous detection costs. (3) Sleep: a smoothed measure of motion below a threshold for a while; wake on a contact or a force; islands are the connected components of the contact graph (union–find), sleeping and waking together. (4) Tolerances: ignore penetrations and closing speeds below a small slop ("Más a fondo": Baumgarte and soft constraints). (5) Drift: renormalize quaternions (4.8). (6) Creep: the velocity a slope adds in one step (MF §16.1.2). **Worked example:** a cannonball of radius 5 cm at 60 Hz against a 10 cm wall tunnels above (0,1 + 0,1) × 60 = 12 m/s, so a 30 m/s shot needs continuous detection.
- **The live figure:** a Rapier sandbox with "break it" toggles: solver iterations, continuous detection, a mass-ratio slider (a heavy box on a light one), slope angle against friction, the sleep threshold with bodies coloured awake or asleep, an island view; a toggle runs the same stack through a worst-first resolver and through Rapier. One knob, two failures: the sleep threshold (too high, a box just past its friction angle freezes forever; too low, nothing sleeps, stacks buzz and cost) and the solver iterations (too few, the stack sinks; too many, the frame bar). Perturbations: a huge impulse; the step slider. Panel: iterations, largest penetration, bodies awake, islands, tunnelled?, ms per step. **Expected:** at 30 m/s, tunnelled without continuous detection and not with it; the other values measured when the figure is built.
- **Traps:** **correction:** MF's sleep measure adds |**v**|² and |**ω**|², quantities of different units, so the threshold depends on the body's size. Smoothing must be per step, not per frame. A sleeping body must still generate contacts so others can wake it (MF). Determinism: sleep and island order change what is computed; same thresholds and same body order on every machine.
- **Exercises:** (1) the fewest solver iterations that hold a ten-crate stack still for 10 s. (2) The continuous-detection threshold for the project's cannonball and wall, verified with the toggle. (3) MF's infinitely rough boxes (fig. 15.6): with sequential resolution, do two boxes pushed together creep apart?
- **Our angle:** Manifold's measured findings: "lo que casi resbala se duerme" (a box just past its friction angle falls asleep in Rapier and stays forever); the bounce comes back 0,484 m instead of *e*² · 2 = 0,5 because restitution acts on the velocity of the step in which it touches, which begins before the contact (3-05); ghost collisions at seams (3.16). En los motores: Catto's Solver2D compares eight solvers on the same stacks.
- **Sources:** MF ch. 16, §15.5, §17.3, ch. 20; G2 §14.3.5.7, §14.4.7.7, §14.4.10. Free: Catto, [_Simulation Islands_](https://box2d.org/posts/2023/10/simulation-islands/), [_Solver2D_](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/), [_Continuous Collision_](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_ContinuousCollision_GDC2013.pdf); Rapier's [integration parameters](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/integration_parameters) and [CCD](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_body_ccd).
- **Reuses:** 1-00's "Más a fondo" "Dormir" and "El solver, de cerca"; 3-05's Rapier findings (the 0,484 m bounce, the sleeping box).
- **Open:** verify Rapier 0.21's solver (substeps, the parameter names) before building the knobs; measure the stack and mass-ratio numbers.

### 4.13 La masa: empujar y agarrar (C)

- **Question:** ¿Cómo se empuja una caja pesada, y cómo se agarra una liviana?
- **Before:** 4.11 (bodies, kinematic by velocity); 4.2 (springs and their bound); 3.9 (picking an object).
- **Leads:** G2 §14.4.8.8 (powered constraints), §14.5.1.4 (a body changes kind: held, then thrown), §14.5.3.1; MF §6.2.2 (anchored springs), §10.3.1 (a spring at a body point gives a force and a torque).
- **Approach:** grabbing is a spring from the hand to a point of the body, damped and capped (MF, G2), so mass matters: heavy things lag and resist. Pushing is a capped force against friction μ*mg*. Holding by teleport (a kinematic "attach") is shown failing: it goes through walls and lifts anything. A "doblar las reglas" Sutileza: Half-Life 2's gravity gun. **Hook:** if the push is a velocity, the ball moves a crate and a stone block alike; a mouse drag that sets a box's position shoves it into a wall.
- **The mathematics:** (1) Pushing: a force *F*max against friction μ*mg* (4.5): it moves iff *F*max > μ*mg*, at (*F*max − μ*mg*)/_m_. (2) Holding as a change of velocity per step: Δ**v** = (**x**target − **x**)/τ + (**v**target − **v**) − **g**_h_, a critically damped spring written in velocity form with τ ≈ 0,12 s (2.3's relaxation), clamped so that |*m*Δ**v**/_h_| ≤ *F*max. 4.2's bound is why it is a relaxation with τ ≫ _h_, not a stiff spring. (3) Grabbed at a point **r**, the box feels **r** × **F** (MF §10.3.1): held by a corner, it swings. (4) Release: dynamic again, keeping its velocity (a throw). **Worked example** (3-06's numbers): a crate of 44,8 kg and a stone block of 1 228,8 kg; μ = 0,55 (wood 0,5 on stone 0,6, averaged) and 0,6; friction 242 N and 7 233 N. A 900 N push moves the crate at (900 − 242)/44,8 ≈ 14,7 m/s² and not the block. A 1 200 N hold lifts the crate (440 N) and not the block (12 054 N).
- **The live figure:** a kinematic paddle moved by velocity and dragged with the mouse, and the mouse grab (pick the crate as in 3.9, then a capped, damped spring toward the cursor's point on a plane); the light crate and the heavy block side by side; toggles: the cap on or off, "attach" (kinematic teleport) on or off, grab by the centre or by a corner. Panel: mass, friction, push, moved?, height held, lag, force used. **Expected:** the crate moves and the block does not; the crate held near the target, the block at 0 cm; "attach" pushes the crate into the wall (penetration above 0).
- **Traps:** a stiff grab spring explodes at a high stiffness for the held mass (4.2's bound), not at a low frame rate: the step is fixed. A kinematic body has infinite mass and pushes through anything. Rapier's own character push (an impulse per step by the character's mass) adds up: Manifold measured a one-tonne block moving like the crate, and later could not reproduce it with the engine's 0,4 kg capsule; measure before stating a number. Friction belongs to the pair (Rapier averages: 0,55, not 0,5).
- **Exercises:** (1) the force needed to move the block (above 7 233 N): set the cap just above it and check. (2) Release when the grab has pulled at full force for half a second (a breakable joint, Unity's `breakForce`). (3) Grab by a corner and predict which way the crate turns (**r** × **F**).
- **Our angle:** "Dónde Manifold eligió otra cosa": neither Rapier's push nor attaching; a capped force against friction, and a capped spring in velocity form to hold (Bitácora 3g). The character of part 5 pushes and grabs exactly so (5.2). Forces enter at a fixed phase, as commands (2.7).
- **Sources:** G2 §14.4.8.8, §14.5.1.4, §14.5.3.1; MF §6.2.2, §10.3.1. Free: [Rapier's rigid bodies](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_bodies) (forces and impulses).
- **Reuses:** 3-06 (la masa): the crate and the block, the 900 N push and 1 200 N hold caps, the spring in velocity form with τ = 0,12 s, the "attach" toggle, the Half-Life 2, Unreal, Unity and Godot comparisons, its exercises. Its figure used a character, which part 4 does not have: a kinematic paddle and the mouse replace it.
- **Open:** nothing beyond the reuse note above (the project uses this in 5.2).

### 4.14 Tirar cosas (P)

- **Question:** ¿Hacia dónde hay que disparar para pegarle a algo lejos?
- **Before:** 4.12 (continuous detection); 4.13 (impulses on bodies); 1.16 (aiming with quaternions); 3.5 and 3.9 (picking a target).
- **Leads:** MA §3.5.1–3.5.5 (the firing solution; drag; iterative targeting), corrected; MF §4.1 (ballistic presets), §17.2.3 (explosions in three phases); GPP ch. 19 (Object Pool); G2 §14.5.3.2–4 (bullets as rays or bodies; grenades bent with extra drag or precomputed arcs; explosions as radial impulses).
- **Approach:** MA's firing solution derived whole (MA leaves it "as an exercise" and gets its code wrong), drawn with its envelope; GPP's pool, with the free list as deterministic state; G2's bent grenades. **Hook:** the turret, now a cannon, must hit what the player clicks; aiming straight at it falls short.
- **The mathematics:** (1) _E_ = _S_ + **û** _st_ + ½ **g** *t*² with **Δ** = _E_ − _S_: **û** = (2**Δ** − **g** *t*²)/(2*st*), and |**û**| = 1 gives |**g**|² *t*⁴ − 4(**g**·**Δ** + *s*²) *t*² + 4|**Δ**|² = 0, a quadratic in _u_ = *t*². (2) Real roots iff (**g**·**Δ** + *s*²)² ≥ |**g**|²|**Δ**|²: the boundary of the reachable set (the envelope); on level ground, _d_ ≤ *s*²/_g_. Two roots: the low and the high arc. (3) **Corrections (MA):** its code takes square roots of the *t*² candidates before checking that they are not negative (a NaN never compares below 0, so the check never fires); its bisection's midpoint is written (max − min)/2 and must be (max + min)/2. (4) The stepped flight (semi-implicit, 2.2) falls ½|**g**| _th_ below the exact parabola; it is exactly the parabola launched at **v**₀ + ½ **g** _h_, so aim with the exact solution **w** and fire **w** − ½ **g** _h_ (if Rapier's step matches: to measure). (5) Explosion: a radial impulse *J*₀ _f_(_r_) **r̂** to the bodies a sphere query finds, _f_ falling linearly to 0 at _R_ (MF's three phases in "Más a fondo"). (6) The pool: fixed slots and a free list threaded through the dead ones (take and release in O(1)); when full, refuse or reuse the oldest (GPP). **Worked examples:** a level target 20 m away, _s_ = 20 m/s, _g_ = 9,81 m/s²: 96,24 *u*² − 1 600 _u_ + 1 600 = 0, so _t_ = 1,034 s (elevation 14,7°) or 3,944 s (75,3°); the longest reach is 40,8 m. MA Ex. 3.13: a ball kicked at 10 m/s along (3/5, 4/5) lands after 1,63 s, 9,79 m away. The stepped 1,034 s flight lands 8,5 cm low.
- **The project:** `cannon.ts` (new): `firingSolution(start, target, speed, g)` returning 0, 1 or 2 {time, direction}, the low one first; `reachable(start, target, speed, g)`. The turret's lock-on (1.16's shortest arc, turning in time as in 2.6) now follows the solution's direction. `pool.ts` (new): `Pool` with `take` and `release`; its free list is world state (hashed, kept by snapshots). Projectiles are Rapier balls with continuous detection on; wind is a force applied every step, drawn per round from the seed. `explosion.ts` (new): `explode(center, j0, radius)` through a sphere query, impulses applied in body-id order. `debug.ts` draws the arc as segments, each ray-cast for the first hit (G2's preview). Input: click a target, fire with a key, a key for low or high. Playable: a firing range in the maze's courtyard, targets on crates of several masses, one explosive shell per round. Console: `cannon.speed 20` and `wind 2 0 0` (world), `show.arc on` (view), `pool.size 16` (startup), `aim x y z` (a command). **Recipe:** `cannon.speed 20; aim 20 0 0` logs `t 1.034 elev 14.7°; t 3.944 elev 75.3°`; `aim 50 0 0` logs `out of reach (max 40.8 m)`; `seed 7; restart; step 900; hash` gives the recorded value in every browser.
- **Traps:** in third person the reticle's ray from the camera and the gun's line differ, and a target under the reticle may be out of the gun's reach (G2); small fast projectiles tunnel without continuous detection (4.12); a pooled projectile's id must not be reused while something still holds it (a generation counter; GPP and ours). Determinism: the free list's order decides the ids, so it is state; explosion impulses in a fixed order; never a square root of a negative (check first). With drag the closed form is inexact: iterative targeting with a trial simulation; with a deterministic engine a trial shot in a snapshot predicts the real one exactly ("Más a fondo").
- **Exercises:** (1) MA Ex. 3.13–3.14: the kicked ball's landing (1,63 s, 9,79 m), and why it might not land there in the game. (2) "Corregí al autor": inputs for which MA's firing code returns NaN. (3) MF Mini-project 4.1: a grenade with extra drag after its first bounce (G2: bending the rules).
- **Our angle:** part 1's turret becomes the cannon (one world that accumulates). The envelope is where the discriminant vanishes: a design tool (put targets just inside it). Determinism makes a trial shot an oracle. 4.1's corrected scaling lets a designer slow a shot on the same arc.
- **Sources:** MA §3.5.1–3.5.5; MF §4.1, §17.2.3; GPP ch. 19; G2 §14.5.3.2–4. Free: [Rapier's CCD](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_body_ccd); Nystrom, [_Object Pool_](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/object-pool.html).
- **Reuses:** nothing from the archive.
- **Open:** measure whether Rapier's stepped flight equals *x*₀ + **v**₀*t* + ½**g**_t_(_t_ + _h_) (substeps would change it) before writing the ½**g**_h_ aiming correction (see the problems).

## Problems found in the curriculum

Each sheet above keeps the curriculum as approved; these are the problems found while designing it.

1. **4.2 uses eigenvalues, which no earlier lesson teaches.** The curriculum's main path for 4.2 needs the eigenvalues of a 2×2 matrix. The sheet teaches them there, from the trace and the determinant (λ² − (tr)λ + det = 0), a short and complete section.
2. **"Eigenvalues on the unit circle while ω*h* ≤ 2" is right, but stability needs ω*h* < 2.** At ω*h* = 2 the semi-implicit step has a double eigenvalue −1 with a Jordan block, and the motion grows linearly. The bound to state is _k_ < 4*m*/*h*² (MF's reader wrote ≤).
3. **4.8 needs the spectral theorem, which no earlier lesson teaches.** Principal axes are its consequence. The sheet states it in the main path and proves it in a "Más a fondo".
4. **The rope bridge of 4.6 cannot carry a Rapier body after 4.11.** Two engines share no contacts, and the curriculum keeps the toy engine "for particles and effects". Proposal: the bridge stays in the toy room (`physics.engine toy`), and a jointed bridge returns in 5.11, where the curriculum already has "a hinged bridge".
5. **4.5 reuses 3-05, which runs on Rapier, but 4.5 comes before Rapier.** Its figure must be rebuilt on the hand-built particle engine and its numbers recomputed; 3-05's Rapier findings (the 0,484 m bounce, the box that falls asleep) move to 4.11–4.12.
6. **4.14's aim is off by ½|**g**|_th_ if it uses the exact parabola.** The stepped flight lands 8,5 cm low after 1 s at 60 Hz. The exact correction (aim the parabola for **v**₀ + ½**g**_h_) depends on how Rapier steps (substeps would change it): to measure before writing.
7. **4.6's "Antes" lists 2.14, but `hash.ts` is born in 2.15.** Harmless in a linear reading; the column should say 2.15.
8. **4.13 reuses 3-06, whose figure needs a character (part 5).** Not a curriculum error, but the reuse is not direct: the sheet replaces the character with a kinematic paddle and the mouse.
