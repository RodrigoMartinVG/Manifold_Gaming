# Roadmap

Where Manifold Gaming is going, how far it got, and what is left. It is updated at the end of every step. The why of each piece is in [the decisions](docs/decisions/README.md); what happened in each step, with its findings and measurements, in [the Bitácora](https://rodrigomartinvg.github.io/Manifold_Gaming/bitacora/) (the site's log, in Spanish).

**Legend:** ✅ done · 🔨 in progress · ⬜ to do · 🔒 a decision is needed first

**Now:** phase 2 is done: levels as text and zones (2a), the ways of moving as modes (2b), capabilities on and off live (2c), platforms that carry (2d), the seams the next phases lean on (2.5), a mannequin with animations mixed by direction (2e, 2e2), the tutorial's deeper pass, and its close: the circuit, a longer level in the Dojo played from start to goal by a test, and the phase's five lessons. Next: phase 3, a world that responds, now in stages (3.0): first 3a, what the program moves made solid (the moving platforms phase 2's close left for it).

They come from a review of the idea drafts on 2026-11-14 (kept outside the repository, like decision 0012's idea bank), which also placed each of their ideas in the phase that first needs it, below.

## What each phase leaves for the next

The question for the start of every step: does it have what it needs, and does it leave what the next ones will need? Reviewed on 2026-11-12, after 1c2. That review moved levels and sensors to phase 2, put snapshots and an extensible input before the network, and added what no phase built (the game's interface, the inventory, dungeons from a seed, where the server runs). Reviewed again on 2026-11-14, with the idea drafts: it added the turning platforms' carry, the stage 2.5 seams, named entities, logical light and sound, the camera against walls, collision poses as data and the networking model. And on 2026-11-15, when phase 2 closed (far phases stay coarse on purpose: each one is broken into stages when it starts, and 4.5 reviews the whole): phase 3's demonstration asked for a guard, which comes in phase 4, so it gets a watcher; phase 3 starts by breaking itself into stages; and it added health and damage (4), budgets for what is drawn (3), the player's settings and accessibility (3), the signaling a browser host needs (6), and two decisions before phase 5 (music, the games' language).

And four questions before writing a step's code: does it run the same in Node, without a screen and without the browsers' trigonometry? Is it a rule that composes, or a special case wired in? Does this phase's demonstration need it (if not, it goes to the idea bank)? Can it be checked in five seconds from the Dojo's console?

## Phase 1 — Physics and a character ✅

- ✅ **1a — The physics engine.** Rapier (its deterministic build) behind a small boundary; bodies as data; gravity as a field, stored as data; the Dojo with physics; the same hash in Chromium, Firefox, WebKit and Node.
- ✅ **Before 1b — The console and the camera, friendlier:** a free camera in the Dojo (drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, `camera.reset`); `help` with the game's examples; console output that can be clicked (examples, rooms, Tab options, suggestions with the whole line corrected); `inspect <id>` and a click on an object to inspect it; `reset [name]`; `vars` grouped by scope; clearer usage messages; `show.stats` with the physics step's time and the number of bodies.
- ✅ **1b — The character and its camera:** a character as data (capsule, character controller: slides along walls, climbs 35 cm steps and 50° slopes, snaps to the ground, pushes cubes), coyote time and jump buffering, its "up" from the gravity (tested walking on a wall with gravity along −Z), player inputs through the session at concrete steps (kept only when they change, part of the hash), keyboard and gamepad turned into a world direction by where the camera looks, a follow camera (`view.camera`), and a classic level in the Dojo (`dojo.level course`).
- ✅ **1c — Replays and ghosts:** a replay is the world's birth recipe, the inputs and console changes with their steps, and the last step's hash, as readable text; links (`#replay=…`, compressed) play it and check the hash at the end; `replay export|play|stop`; ghosts (`ghost`, `#ghost=…`) run another world in step, next to the player. The input gained "where to look" (`face`) before the format was fixed, for `view.camera aim`. A run driven with the keys in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit replays to the same hash in Node.
- ✅ **1c2 — Turning and the camera:** a second key scheme, `controls.scheme tank`: W and S go where the character looks, A and D turn it on its own axis, Q and E (or Alt with A and D) go to its sides. The input gains `ahead`, `side` and `turn`, resolved in the simulation (where the character looks is the world's), turning without trigonometry. Cameras: `follow` on a leash (going to a side turns it), a new `chase` that comes back behind the character, the gamepad's right stick, and aim's vertical mouse the right way round. Replays carry a version (`# Manifold replay 2`) and write inputs as words; a little negative input rounded to −0 no longer gives another hash on replay.
- ✅ **1d — The world's state that travels:** what a player asks for is named values (buttons, axes, vectors) of declared actions: the core declares the character's, each game its own with its namespace (the Dojo's first: `dojo.throw`, F). The session lets in only what was declared, and a replay that brings anything else is refused. The world remembers the previous step's inputs (`pressed`). Snapshots as decision 0004 defined them (the components, Rapier's snapshot, the randomness, the inputs): restored, the same hash on every step, in Chromium, Firefox, WebKit and Node; `snapshot` and `snapshot load` in the console, and the replay still holds after going back. The hash now counts the id counter. One version for replays and snapshots (3). Measured: the course with 12 cubes, 32 KB of physics and 11 KB of entities, 0.7 ms to save.
- ✅ **1e — The networking skeleton** (decision 0000, rule 6): a new package, `@manifold/net` (no screen, no transport of its own). The server keeps the match, decides at which step each thing enters and sends a tick per step, with the hash every second; every client simulates its own copy and checks it. **Every game plays through it**, with a server in the same page when alone, so every match also checks determinism while it is played. `players.join` and `players.leave` as world data (in the course, a character for each player); who changes the world is the game's (`mayChange`; the Dojo lets everybody); joining a started match with a snapshot and the history; snapshots, restarts and replays are the server's. Two tabs of the same browser play one match (`net open`, `net join`, through a `BroadcastChannel`). Measured: through the server 0.72 ms per step against 0.45 alone (two worlds); a tick, about 52 characters. Left for phase 6, on purpose: internet, prediction, and smoothing what arrives in bursts.
- ✅ **1f — Closing the phase:** a lint rule that keeps out of the simulation (the core, the network, a game's rules) what each JavaScript engine computes its own way (`Math.sin`, `cos`, `atan2`, `hypot`, `exp`, `pow`, `**`), `Math.random` and `quat.fromAxisAngle`; it found the last two uses, the spin of entities without a body, which now turns without trigonometry. The physics lab promised by decision 0010: `show.vectors`, `show.contacts` (read from Rapier through the boundary), `show.gravity`. Rapier in the live examples (4.4 MB, 1.7 MB compressed: each lesson now fetches only the libraries it imports), and the phase's five lessons: a physics engine, a character, replays and ghosts, snapshots, a server and a client — with tests that read what each lesson shows, not only that it runs.
- ✅ **The demonstration:** a character runs and jumps in a classic level, and its replay gives the same hash — in Chromium, Firefox, WebKit and Node. The seed of the platformer.

## Phase 2 — Capabilities ✅

- ✅ **2a — Levels as data, zones, a level's loop:** a level format of our own (text, versioned, errors with their line; an importer from Blender or an editor will write this same text later), the course moved out of the code into `games/dojo/levels/course.level`; zones that notice who comes in and leaves (in their own axes, without Rapier); the Dojo's loop: a checkpoint, the void that brings you back, the goal with its time and falls, kept as the character's `game` data; notices on screen; `show.sensors`; and our own sine and cosine in degrees, the same bits everywhere.
- ✅ **2b — The composition protocol:** one mode of moving at each step (walking, in the air), chosen in one place (where 2c's capabilities will come in), each one steering the velocity along the ground its own way; contributions that add up whatever the mode (gravity, and what the game makes the character carry: a wind, a belt, 2d's platforms). The movement's numbers are data of each game (`movement`: walk, air control, jump with coyote time and buffer, turn); `dojo.air` changes the air control live (0: real physics, the jump's arc cannot be steered).
- ✅ **Where the art and the sound come from** — written in 2.5c ([decision 0015](docs/decisions/0015-art-and-sound.md)): level pieces and mechanisms generated with code (the level format, parametric geometry, Blender run from the command line by scripts in the repository; never by hand); characters and animations from CC0 packs, normalized by a script; sound synthesized by code; every file in a list with its source and license, checked by a test.
- ✅ **2c — Capabilities:** climbing (a wall noticed with a ray from the feet; a hop at the top; a jump off it), gliding (a slow fall, more steering) and a double jump, each with its numbers as data and turned on and off per character (`can`), live; they come in where the mode is chosen. The course gained a 6 m tower, an island in the void and three stars, each reachable only with its capability (tested); the room `capabilities` has them all on. Seen in the Dojo: `dojo.climb`, `dojo.glide`, `dojo.doublejump`.
- ✅ **2d — Moving platforms:** boxes in the level format that go back and forth (`move 9 0 0 5`) or turn (`spin y 40`), as kinematic bodies whose place is a function of the step: they never drift, and phase 6 can re-simulate them. A ray down finds the one under the character; the engine carries it exactly as far as the platform takes the point where it stands (its arm turned with the physics' own turn, without the browsers' trigonometry), turns it with it, and a jump or a walk off the edge leaves with its velocity. **V1's "the controller gets stuck on kinematic bodies", explained:** measured with Rapier alone, on a kinematic body (still or moving, by velocity or by position) the character controller stands with no gap and every movement along it comes out zero; on the same body, fixed, it walks. So, while the controller computes, the platform under the character is fixed for it, and kinematic again (with its velocities) before the physics steps. The course gained a carousel by the start and a ferry over the void; the `follow` and `chase` cameras go along with what the platform carries, without turning with it (the character turns, and its visor goes round; a first version turned the camera too, and the character looked the same on the screen while the world went round it), while `aim`, which is where the character looks, turns with it. For that, the engine keeps the turn the platforms gave each character (`carriedTurn`) and reads where it is told to look (`face`) inside it, as Quake III's "delta angles": told the same thing, a turning platform still turns it, exactly, in the simulation; the aiming camera reads that turn from the world, and whoever knows where it wants a character to look in the world (the camera; the bots of phase 4) asks for `faceFor(character, direction)`. (A first fix turned the camera frame by frame, imitating the platform: it jittered, every camera and bot would have had to repeat it, and replays grew every step.) the console gained `inspect me`, `inspect it` and `watch` (values pinned on screen while playing). Measured: standing on the carousel, 0.09–0.10 ms per step against 0.08 on the floor (Node, the course); after two whole turns, the character within 1 mm of where it was; a run on the carousel recorded in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit gives the same hash in Node. Closing it, a review of every stage's report in the Bitácora, looking for patches, fixed two causes: a body put somewhere (back at a checkpoint, `dojo.goto`) left its shape behind until the next step, so the character controller computed that step from where it had been (it sank 2.7 mm into the floor); and the browser tests waited on the clock, not on the world (now they wait for its steps, and every test has one time budget, written once). What it found and left open went to 2.5, each piece where it belongs. Still open from 2c: getting stuck on the line between two steps (2.5b).
- ✅ **2.5 — The seams the next phases lean on** (before 2e). What phases 3 and 4 will build on, changed now, while there is one game and nothing saved to protect. The foundations stay; the refactors keep every reference replay's hash. It also takes what the review for patches found at the end of 2d (2026-11-14): fixes that worked around a cause instead of removing it, each written below where it belongs.

## Phase 3 — A world that responds 🔨

The biggest phase so far, broken into stages (3.0) by what depends on what: what the program moves made solid first, since elevators, doors and bridges lean on it; then the level format that names things, gives surfaces their materials and zones their parameters, which signals, traction and the light all read; then mechanisms, the light with its watcher, and things that break, which together make the phase's demonstration (**breaking a lamp changes what a watcher sees**); and after it, the stages that lean on each other least. Each stage is shown in the Dojo and checked by tests, as phase 2's were; one that chooses something with a long tail (the signals, the saved game's format) writes a decision first; and each one's report notes the lessons its ideas will need, written at the phase's close (decision 0013).

## Phase 7 — Strange worlds ⬜

- ⬜ Gravity fields that change from place to place (planets, tori), from attractors (a sphere, a cylinder or a curve, any mesh). **Walking on walls and ceilings is adhesion, not gravity:** gravity is the space's (everything falls that way), adhesion the contact's (only the character sticks; a cube or a thrown ball still falls to the real floor, and the other players are not pulled). So the character gets its own "up", apart from the gravity's, and adhesion is one more contribution (grip along the wall, hold on ceilings, suction); `wallwalk` is a mode, unlike `climb` (which keeps the world's "up"), and it turns concave and convex corners. Where the character looks and the camera are carried along when "up" changes, without trigonometry (decision 0000: also standing still, and with an opposite "up").
- ⬜ A gluing system: spaces that wrap around, or come back mirrored (physics, drawing and networking across the seams). The spatial queries routed through the world since 2.5 learn to cross the seams; a mirrored seam flips each character's handedness (so right stays right on screen), and the camera's leash continues across a seam.
- ⬜ Two games of the second wave, chosen from the idea bank when the base is firm.

## Phase 8 — Power for one player (optional) ⬜

What the verification costs, given back when nobody needs it. Playing alone, two full copies of the world run in the page today: the server's, which counts, and the client's, which is drawn (1e measured 0.72 ms per step against 0.45 with one; with thousands of bodies, almost double). They are what checks determinism while playing, and what makes a replay or a networked match trustworthy. A single-player game that wants thousands of bodies with consequences may give that up, knowingly, and keep everything else: the same engine and the same game code, with a profile chosen, not programmed (2.5b). The verified profile stays the default, and the tests require it.

- ⬜ **The local profile**, for the games that declare it (a startup value: changing it restarts the world; never in a networked match): the inputs go the same path, without the second copy that verifies; the hash is computed when asked for, not compared.
- ⬜ A faster physics build for that profile (Rapier's SIMD one, measured first: it is not deterministic across machines), handed to the world like any backend; and the physics in a worker if the measurements ask (messages with transferable arrays: GitHub Pages cannot send the headers that shared memory needs).
- ⬜ Breakables that say so promote their fragments (phase 3's cosmetic layer) to real bodies, with consequences: they stop shots, pile up, push the character; thousands of them drawn as instances of one mesh.
- ⬜ What is lost, said where it is lost: replays and ghosts by inputs play only on the machine that recorded them (they carry their profile, and elsewhere they are refused); ghosts as strips of poses (4.5) still travel; no networked match.
- ⬜ **The demonstration:** a structure that falls into thousands of real bodies, at 60 frames per second on a modest machine; and, in the Dojo, the same room in both profiles, to compare what each one costs and gives.

## Open questions

- ✅ **Where the art and the sound come from** — [decision 0015](docs/decisions/0015-art-and-sound.md).
- ✅ **The networking model, written down** — [decision 0016](docs/decisions/0016-the-networking-model.md): the server orders the inputs and every copy simulates them; online, each one predicts only its own character and reconciles it by re-simulating only it; the others are seen at the last confirmed step, in the past, which is why the server judges hits with a pose history (phase 4), capped at 200–250 ms.
- ✅ **Phase 5's scope** — [decision 0017](docs/decisions/0017-phase-5-scope.md): three compact games, of 10 to 15 minutes each.
- 🔒 **Where the server runs** — before phase 6. 1e keeps it open by running in Node and in a browser. A proposal is in phase 6.
- ⬜ **Replays and ghosts across versions** — a replay only plays on the engine version that recorded it, and the site keeps only the latest one. Before the platformer shares ghosts (phase 5): keep each version published, or ghosts as recorded positions (they survive the changes, but are not checked), with replays by inputs for what must be exact. Proposal (4.5): ghosts as strips of quantized poses (`x y z yaw mode` every 3 or 4 steps, under 2 KB a minute).
- ⬜ **Music** — decision 0015 covers the art and the sounds, not music: generated like the sounds, CC0, or none. Before phase 5.
- ⬜ **The games' language** — the site is in Spanish and the console in English (0011); the games' own words (menus, dialogue, the HUD) have no decision: Spanish, English, or both. Before phase 5, or before phase 3's interface if it needs words.
- ⬜ **Touch controls** — the site works on phones; the games do not, yet. Before phase 5: touch controls, or keyboard and gamepad only. Proposal: keyboard and gamepad only until phase 6.
- ✅ **Lessons per step or per phase** — per phase, at its close (decision 0013, which changes 0005's "with each step" on purpose). Each step's report in the Bitácora notes the lessons its ideas will need.
- ⬜ **Firefox and WebKit in continuous integration** — since 2.5a, continuous integration runs the determinism check in the three (the reference replays, played without drawing). Their physics tests, which draw, still run only locally, until their WebGL is checked on Linux.
- ⬜ **The hash on every step** — 1.8 ms with 200 bodies, three times a physics step (1a); in 2.5a's stress room, 3.5–6.5 of Rapier's steps, under a budget of 8. The server compares it every second (1e); phase 6 may add a quick hash of the entities every few steps.
- ⬜ **What the verification costs** — measured in 2.5a, in the stress room: playing alone (the server's copy, which verifies, and the one that is drawn) costs 2.06–2.25 copies; the hash, once a second, is in that. It decides whether the page's server goes to a worker (4), and what phase 8 can promise: about half of the simulation's time.
- ⬜ **Rapier's usual build** — whether it would also be deterministic across browsers was not measured; the deterministic one was chosen so as not to depend on it.
- ✅ **Shorter replay links** — 2.5c: 30 s on the course with the camera turning was a link of 5,803 characters; with directions in whole degrees that change only past 1°, 2,712 (aiming: 10,219, now 4,787).
