# Roadmap

Where Manifold Gaming is going, how far it got, and what is left. It is updated at the end of every step. The why of each piece is in [the decisions](docs/decisions/README.md); what happened in each step, with its findings and measurements, in [the Bitácora](https://rodrigomartinvg.github.io/Manifold_Gaming/bitacora/) (the site's log, in Spanish).

**Legend:** ✅ done · 🔨 in progress · ⬜ to do · 🔒 a decision is needed first

**Now:**

- Phases 0 to 5 are done: the engine, the Dojo and three games (the tower, the fortress, the wall); each stage below links to its report in the Bitácora, which tells what it did and measured.
- Stage 5.5, the shape before the forest ([decision 0033](docs/decisions/0033-the-shape-before-the-forest.md)), is done: every piece one place, guarded by default, the proofs made to scale, the engine at version 50.
- Next: the forest ([decision 0032](docs/decisions/0032-the-forest.md)), from F0's measures; phase 6, networking and levels, after it.
- The tutorial goes on in its own plan, [docs/tutorial-roadmap.md](docs/tutorial-roadmap.md).
- What is decided and why: [docs/decisions](docs/decisions/README.md).

After phase 7, an optional **phase 8** gives a single-player game the power the verification takes; its seams are planted where they are cheap and useful anyway (2.5b, 3, 4). They come from a review of the idea drafts on 2026-11-14 (kept outside the repository, like decision 0012's idea bank), which also placed each of their ideas in the phase that first needs it, below.

## What each phase leaves for the next

The question for the start of every step: does it have what it needs, and does it leave what the next ones will need? Reviewed after 1c2 (2026-11-12), with the idea drafts (11-14), when phases 2 and 3 closed (11-15), before 4.0 (11-16), when 4.5 was broken into stages and after it, against five compendiums of techniques (11-17), when phase 5 closed (11-18), and before the forest (decision 0032); what each review moved is in the Bitácora and the decisions.

And four questions before writing a step's code: does it run the same in Node, without a screen and without the browsers' trigonometry? Is it a rule that composes, or a special case wired in? Does this phase's demonstration need it (if not, it goes to the idea bank)? Can it be checked in five seconds from the Dojo's console?

## The new tutorial 🔨

Its own track from now on ([decision 0021](docs/decisions/0021-a-new-tutorial.md), proposed): one project that grows lesson by lesson and ends each part in something played, the mathematics when the game asks for it (whole, typeset, precise), free to teach ahead of the engine; the current tutorial is archived and, with Manifold Lab, is raw material.

- ✅ **The pilot:** the introduction, part 1's page and its first lessons, with what they needed: KaTeX in the site's Markdown; the project as one tree of modules, a folder per step with only what it changes (`src/course/`, `src/course.ts`), the files a lesson did not touch shown quieter; a test that puts each step's project together and type-checks it; the lesson's shape as a test (the main path at least 60% of the page, the layers short); browser tests that read what each lesson shows. The author's reading added concept lessons beside the project's (Coordenadas, La proyección, Vectores), and asked for a curriculum grounded in the reference books.
- ✅ **The curriculum** ([`docs/tutorial-curriculum.md`](docs/tutorial-curriculum.md), approved): 146 lessons in ten parts, from the books weighed together, with one set of conventions; and **a design sheet for every lesson** ([`docs/tutorial-lessons/`](docs/tutorial-lessons/)), with the decisions across parts that wait for the author.
- ✅ **The infrastructure:** the lab kit for the live figures ([`site/src/lab/`](site/src/lab/), imported as `'lab'`), the notation page and "TypeScript para quien sabe JavaScript"; the new tutorial's code checked with `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, as the engine is.
- ✅ **The archive:** the old tutorial moved, as it is, to `/tutorial/archivo/`, each page with a banner and its old address sent there; the new one took the Tutorial section (and its pilot's addresses, `/tutorial-nuevo/…`, are sent too). A test reads every link of the site.
- ✅ **The decisions across parts** (the sheets' review, [`docs/tutorial-lessons/README.md`](docs/tutorial-lessons/README.md)), taken as recommended: gravity as a field from 2.8, `trig.ts` in degrees (an exception to the notation's radians, stated), the input record with a payload, one console grammar, our own zones, effects only drawn, 5.2's constant-acceleration motor, one `view/` folder. ## Phase 6 — Networking and levels ⬜

- ⬜ Networked matches: the shooter online, on top of 1e's path: prediction, reconciliation (re-simulating only one's own, decision 0000), lag compensation — following the model written in 2.5c ([decision 0016](docs/decisions/0016-the-networking-model.md): the server orders the inputs, everyone simulates, each predicts only its own character and reconciles it by re-simulating only it); the case of the cubes a character pushes; lag compensation capped (200–250 ms). Re-simulating one's own character asks where what carries it or stops it was at those steps: a ferry's, a door's and a lift's place are functions of the step (2d, 3c); a spinning platform's turn is Rapier's (deterministic, measured in 3a), kept in a history or made a function, as measured then. Three pieces the model of 0016 will need, from the compendiums of 2026-11-17: the client's clock kept by how many of its inputs wait at the server (its fixed step 1 to 2% faster or slower, never a jump); a correction after reconciling drawn away, not at once (the difference eased out in about 0.1 s, or put there at once past a few meters); and each input sent again in the next packets, so that one lost does not stall the match.
- 🔒 Where the server runs (decision 0002: Pages only serves files): a hosted server, or one of the browsers as the host. Proposal: one player's tab as the server through WebRTC (free from Pages), and the same package in Node where nobody should have the host's advantage. Part of the decision (found when phase 2 closed): even a tab as the host needs a signaling channel for two browsers to find each other, and, behind some networks, a relay (TURN); GitHub Pages gives neither. A small hosted service, a public one, or the offer and answer passed by hand in a link.
- 🔒 Who can change the world in a networked match (the permissions left open by decision 0010; 1e leaves a first rule). Proposal: in competitive games the console does not touch the world; each command's permissions shown in `help`.
- ⬜ A networking lab in the Dojo: `show.net` (packets, the steps between client and server), `net.loss`, `net.jitter`. A quick hash of the entities every few steps, besides the full one every second. Binary encoding only if something needs it (joining a started match: the citadel's join is 475 KB of physics and 259 KB of entities as text, 4.5f).
- ⬜ A level editor, on top of phase 2's level format and phase 3's console commands (dragging an object emits `set`; saving is `level.export`, 3j).

## Phase 7 — Strange worlds ⬜

- ⬜ (What assumes "up" is +Y today, by system: [the 4.5 audit](docs/audits/up-is-y.md); the navigation, the guards, the input and ghost formats and maps are the structural ones.) Gravity fields that change from place to place (planets, tori), from attractors (a sphere, a cylinder or a curve, any mesh). **Walking on walls and ceilings is adhesion, not gravity:** gravity is the space's (everything falls that way), adhesion the contact's (only the character sticks; a cube or a thrown ball still falls to the real floor, and the other players are not pulled). So the character gets its own "up", apart from the gravity's, and adhesion is one more contribution (grip along the wall, hold on ceilings, suction); `wallwalk` is a mode, unlike `climb` (which keeps the world's "up"), and it turns concave and convex corners. Where the character looks and the camera are carried along when "up" changes, without trigonometry (decision 0000: also standing still, and with an opposite "up").
- ⬜ A gluing system: spaces that wrap around, or come back mirrored (physics, drawing and networking across the seams). The spatial queries routed through the world since 2.5 learn to cross the seams; a mirrored seam flips each character's handedness (so right stays right on screen), and the camera's leash continues across a seam.
- ⬜ Two games of the second wave, chosen from the idea bank when the base is firm.

## Phase 8 — Power for one player (optional) ⬜

What the verification costs, given back when nobody needs it. Playing alone, two full copies of the world run in the page today: the server's, which counts, and the client's, which is drawn (1e measured 0.72 ms per step against 0.45 with one; with thousands of bodies, almost double). They are what checks determinism while playing, and what makes a replay or a networked match trustworthy. A single-player game that wants thousands of bodies with consequences may give that up, knowingly, and keep everything else: the same engine and the same game code, with a profile chosen, not programmed (2.5b). The verified profile stays the default, and the tests require it.

- ⬜ **The local profile**, for the games that declare it (a startup value: changing it restarts the world; never in a networked match): the inputs go the same path, without the second copy that verifies; the hash is computed when asked for, not compared.
- ⬜ A faster physics build for that profile (Rapier's SIMD one, measured first: it is not deterministic across machines), handed to the world like any backend; and the physics in a worker if the measurements ask (messages with transferable arrays: GitHub Pages cannot send the headers that shared memory needs).
- ⬜ Repeated pieces drawn as instances, when a level's draw calls ask for it (4.5f measured the lights as the cost at 250 draw calls; instancing waits for a level near a thousand): `BatchedMesh` rather than `InstancedMesh` for the kit (pieces of several shapes and one material in one draw, each culled on its own). And the collision events every dynamic body reports since 4.5's close (a hit only as a contact begins), measured with a thousand bodies. Breakables that say so promote their fragments (phase 3's cosmetic layer) to real bodies, with consequences: they stop shots, pile up, push the character; thousands of them drawn as instances of one mesh.
- ⬜ What is lost, said where it is lost: replays and ghosts by inputs play only on the machine that recorded them (they carry their profile, and elsewhere they are refused); ghosts as strips of poses (4.5) still travel; no networked match.
- ⬜ **The demonstration:** a structure that falls into thousands of real bodies, at 60 frames per second on a modest machine; and, in the Dojo, the same room in both profiles, to compare what each one costs and gives.
